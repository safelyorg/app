Safely vs Norton

Two different tools for two different risks. Here is what each one checks, and where they overlap.

The short answer

Norton protects your device and warns about scam messages and websites. Safely checks the supplier behind a B2B listing. They look at different things, and many buyers use both.

What Norton does

Norton is a security suite. Its core job is to protect your computer from malware, viruses and other harmful files. Norton also sells scam protection that uses AI to look at messages, links and websites for signs of a scam.

That is useful when the danger is a phishing link, a fake login page or a harmful download.

What Safely does

Safely is a Chrome extension for buyers on B2B marketplaces. When you open a supplier page, it reads the listing, the seller and the company, and runs 15 signals on them. These include registration consistency, price, account age, contact details and community fraud reports. It then shows a 0 to 100 risk score in about 10 seconds, with the evidence behind it.

The risk Safely looks for is a supplier who looks real on a trusted marketplace but is not. Nothing has to be downloaded for that kind of scam to work, so there may be no file or link for a device tool to flag.

Which one do you need?

If you buy from suppliers online, the two do not replace each other. Keep your device protected, and check each new supplier before you send an order or a deposit. A device tool cannot tell you that a supplier's registration details do not match, and Safely cannot clean a virus off your laptop.

Safely gives a risk score from 0 to 100 as guidance. It is not a guarantee, and it does not replace calling the supplier on a phone number you already had.
